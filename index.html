Chapter I  ·  Tanzania

The Great
Migration

Every year, 1.5 million wildebeest thunder across the Serengeti plains in the largest overland migration on Earth. They follow the rains, cross crocodile-infested rivers, and outrun lions, driven by a force older than memory.

The Serengeti is not merely a place. It is a pulse, the heartbeat of an entire ecosystem stretching from Tanzania to Kenya, sustaining more species of large mammals than anywhere else on Earth.

"1.5 million wildebeest. 500,000 gazelles. One of nature's most extraordinary spectacles."

Chapter II  ·  Rwanda & Uganda

Mountain
Gorilla

In the misty highlands of Central Africa lives one of our closest relatives sharing 98.3% of our DNA. The mountain gorilla moves through ancient rainforests with a quiet intelligence that is unmistakably familiar.

Family is everything to a gorilla. A silverback spends his days protecting, teaching, and leading his group through a forest that has existed for millennia.

Only 1,063 mountain gorillas remain on Earth. Every single one matters.

Chapter III  ·  Savanna

Built for
Speed

At 112 kilometres per hour, the cheetah is the fastest land animal to have ever lived. She accelerates from zero to full sprint in three seconds, a feat of biological engineering that took millions of years to perfect.

Yet speed alone cannot outrun the forces threatening her survival. Habitat loss, human conflict, and the illegal wildlife trade have reduced cheetah populations by 90% in a single century.

Fewer than 7,000 cheetahs remain in the wild. The clock is running out.

Chapter IV  ·  Across Africa

The Architects
of Africa

Elephants do not merely live in Africa - they shape it. They knock down trees to create clearings, dig waterholes in dry riverbeds, and carry seeds across hundreds of kilometres. Remove the elephant, and you remove the architect of an entire ecosystem.

They grieve their dead. They comfort their young. They remember paths taken decades ago. In their eyes, you see something ancient and wise. Something that was roaming this Earth long before we arrived.

"An elephant never forgets. The question is: will we remember them in time?"
